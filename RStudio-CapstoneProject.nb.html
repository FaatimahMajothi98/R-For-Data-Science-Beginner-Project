Queen Elizabeth II’s Death, Sports and Betting Stocks: A Google Trends Analysis

By Faatimah Majothi

In the United Kingdom, sports are a big part of the entertainment industry. Football is famously one of the most popular sports thanks to championships and leagues featuring teams from many major towns and cities across the country. Watching and attending games, betting on scores and keeping track of favourite teams and players are familiar activities to many, if not most British people. As a result, betting companies have many branches in most cities, towns, and even villages across the country.

The royal family is also a topic of great interest across the nation. Queen Elizabeth II was arguably the most iconic British royal in the country, and was very popular during her reign until her death in 2022. After her death on the 9th of September in 2022, the national news outlets were flooded with information relating to her, and a mourning period of ten days was announced.

In this project we will be taking a look at how the Queen’s death and consequential national mourning period affected the British public.

We will look at three of the most popular sports:

  • Football

  • Tennis

  • Horse races

We will then go on to investigate the effects of the event on the stock prices of some of the betting companies that have a big presence in the United Kingdom. The three betting company stocks we will investigate are not based in the UK, but have many branches and a strong footing in the country.

The stocks we have selected are:

  • [FLUT] Flutter Entertainment plc (Owns Betfair and PaddyPower, both common in Britain)

  • [CZR] Caesars Entertainment Inc (Owns William Hill, previously Britain’s leading betting company)

  • [ENT.L] Entain PLC (Owns Ladbrokes and Coral, both common in Britain)

To begin with, we create a dataframe with the gtrend data for our key searches.

# Create DataFrame of gtrends
uk_sports <-  gtrends(keyword=c('football', 'tennis', 'horse racing'), geo = 'GB', time = '2021-01-01 2023-12-31')

We then create a dataframe containing the closing prices of the stocks we selected.

# Create individual data frames for each stock
flut_df <- data.frame(Date = as.Date(row.names(as.data.frame(FLUT))), Close1 = FLUT$FLUT.Close)
czr_df <- data.frame(Date = as.Date(row.names(as.data.frame(CZR))), Close2 = CZR$CZR.Close)
ent_df <- data.frame(Date = as.Date(row.names(as.data.frame(ENT.L))), Close3 = ENT.L$ENT.L.Close)

# Merge all data frames by Date
all_stocks <- list(flut_df, czr_df, ent_df) %>%
  reduce(full_join, by = 'Date')

# Reshape for plotting
long_stocks <- all_stocks %>%
  pivot_longer(-Date, names_to = 'Stock', values_to = 'Close')

We can then look at the closing prices of the stocks we have selected. The graph below shows the prices for the stocks between 2020 to 2025.

# Filter dates and visualise
long_stocks %>%
  filter(Date >= as.Date('2015-01-01') & Date <= as.Date('2025-12-31')) %>%
  ggplot() +
  geom_line(aes(x = Date, y = Close, color = Stock)) +
  scale_color_discrete(name = 'Company', labels = c('Flutter Ent.', 'Caesars Ent.', 'Entain PLC')) +
  labs(title = 'Stock Prices of Streaming Services', y = 'Stock Price')

We can “zoom in” on this graph to take a closer look at the years 2021-2023.

long_stocks %>%

Both graphs display the relatively higher volatility of Caesars Entertainment. This was perhaps due to higher stock prices and popularity at the time. Deeper research revealed that the three companies have different areas of focus. Flutter Ent. is an online betting company, while Entain PLC is a sports-betting and gaming company. Caesars Ent. differs in that it operates as a gaming and hospitality company in the United States. We can infer from this that Caesars Ent. has higher volatility as well as stock prices because of its non-sports related earnings. Their ownership of William Hill and their provision of online betting services makes them relevant to this analysis.

# Remove missing vales
trends_over_time <- uk_sports$interest_over_time %>% replace_na(list(hits = 0))

# Visualise
trends_over_time %>% ggplot() + geom_line(aes(date, hits, color = keyword)) +
  scale_color_discrete(name = 'Sport', labels = c('Football','Tennis','Horse Races' )) + 
  labs(title = 'Google Trend Data For Sports', y = 'Hits (Normalized to be between 0 and 1000
       ', x = 'Date', subtitle = 'Search Trends in the United Kingdom')

Immediately, we notice a pattern in the Google search trends for Horse Races and Football. Both sports appear to have significant spikes of interest or disinterest around the middle months of each year displayed on the graph.To understand these patterns, we will have to investigate further.

Through basic external research, we can associate the peaks of interest in horse racing with the major UK horse racing festivals, such as the Royal Ascot and the Cheltenham Festival, which run annually around the April-June period.

Similarly, the UK Premier League football tournament (which runs annually around the August-May period) coincides with the spikes in search trend data around the middle of the years featured on our graph. The highest peak of interest in football in the UK coincides with the September of 2022. This is a notable point for further investigation, as the Queen’s death occurred around that time period. It is worth noting that there is a plunge in hits just before this peak of interest.

Lastly, the search hits for Tennis have few significant peaks and troughs in the trend graph. There appears to be a pattern of very small peaks in the first quarter of each year, which according to external research, can be linked with the British tennis season during which multiple major tennis events are held.

With this overview of the trends over the years before and after the Queen’s death in mind, we can now zoom in on the search trend data closer to the time of her death in September 2022. For ease of visibility, we will zoom in on the 9th of September and the 30 days before and after.

# Filter dates
trends_over_time <- uk_sports$interest_over_time  %>% replace_na(list(hits = 0)) %>%
  filter(date >= as.Date('2022-08-10') & date <= as.Date('2022-10-09'))

# Visualise
trends_over_time %>% ggplot() + geom_line(aes(date, hits, color = keyword)) +
  scale_color_discrete(name = 'Sport', labels = c('Football','Tennis','Horse Races' )) + 
  labs(title = 'Google Trend Data For Sports', y = 'Hits (Normalized to be between 0 and 1000

We can now see that both horse racing and football had a decrease in hits around the time of Queen Elizabeth II’s death, though interest in tennis did not have any significant change. However, we cannot yet determine whether the Queen’s death was the sole cause for the drop in interest in the two sports.
